# FrontStreet — Sales, Marketing & Go-To-Market Plan
### Domain: frontstreetapp.com

FrontStreet's primary buyer is a Black visual artist in Boston — active on Instagram, selling work through DMs and pop-ups, and frustrated by the four-tool stack that doesn't talk to each other. The lead GTM motion is **founder-led community entry**: George Freeman goes person-to-person, event-to-event, institution-to-institution in Boston's Black arts ecosystem to build supply (artists) before opening to demand (collectors). The revenue target is $2,500 MRR within 12 months of launch — driven by Pro subscriptions, boosted listings, and art sale commissions sequenced across three phases.

---

## 1. GTM Strategy Overview

**Primary Motion: Founder-Led Community Entry + Physical Events**

FrontStreet's moat is cultural authenticity. The research confirms this explicitly — a platform built by a Black Boston artist carries trust that Silicon Valley competitors cannot purchase. That trust is deployed in person first: George attends, speaks at, and hosts physical events in Boston's Black arts community, demonstrating the product, onboarding artists on the spot, and letting word of mouth do the work that advertising cannot. This is the only motion that solves the chicken-and-egg problem (artists need collectors, collectors need artists) without requiring a marketing budget. It also reflects how this customer actually discovers tools: through trusted people in their network, not Google ads or cold email.

**Secondary Motions:**

- **Build in Public (Instagram + TikTok):** The process of a Black artist building a platform for Black artists is a story worth telling — behind-the-scenes development content, artist spotlights, and transparent milestones reach potential users where they already spend time. This is zero-cost content that doubles as community recruitment.
- **Email Newsletter (Beehiiv free tier):** A weekly or bi-weekly email to the waitlist and early community — artist spotlights, upcoming Boston events, platform updates — builds the habit of hearing from FrontStreet before the product is live. The list is the asset.
- **Institutional Partnerships:** Direct outreach to BAMS Fest, NCAAA, GRACC, AAMARP, and Artists for Humanity (ProBlak) to position FrontStreet as the digital infrastructure those organisations' artists are missing. One partnership announcement generates more credibility than six months of paid ads.

**What We Are NOT Doing (and Why):**

- **Paid social ads (Meta/Instagram):** At $500/mo total budget, paid social for a marketplace requires both artist and collector audiences be reached simultaneously — a two-sided problem that doubles the minimum viable ad spend. The budget doesn't stretch there, and organic community presence will build the early user base more durably.
- **Cold email outreach:** The target user (Black Boston artists) is reached through community, not inbox. Cold email in a trust-driven cultural category signals the opposite of authenticity. The research explicitly identifies cultural trust as the primary moat — a cold B2B sequence undermines it on first contact.
- **SEO as a primary channel in Year 1:** The product is hyper-local and the initial user base will be acquired through events and relationships, not search. SEO content is built in parallel but shouldn't consume founder time in the first 6 months. The keyword opportunity exists but is a 6–12 month compounding play, not a launch accelerant.

## 2. Ideal Customer Profile (Sales Lens)

### Segment A — The Primary Target: Boston Black Visual Artist

**Firmographics:**
- Solo visual artist or very small studio (1–3 people)
- Revenue from art: $10,000–$75,000/year (part-time to full-time artist)
- Geography: Boston metro — Roxbury, Dorchester, Mattapan, South End, Jamaica Plain, Cambridge
- Works in: painting, photography, mixed media, sculpture, illustration, digital art

**Technographics:**
- Active Instagram account (primary portfolio tool), typically 500–10,000 followers
- Sells through: Instagram DMs, Etsy, personal website (often Squarespace or Wix), or at physical events
- Uses Eventbrite or Facebook Events to list their shows
- Has a smartphone (iOS or Android) as primary computing device for social/marketing
- Not a developer — evaluates tools by simplicity and visual quality, not feature depth

**Behavioral Signals — They're Ready:**
- Posts "link in bio for available work" regularly on Instagram
- Complains publicly about Eventbrite fees or Saatchi Art's commission in comments or Stories
- Regularly RSVPs to or hosts local art events (BAMS Fest, gallery openings, open studios)
- Has tagged their work as available for sale in Instagram posts
- Has expressed frustration with Instagram's algorithmic reach suppression
- Is listed in AAMARP, Artists for Humanity, Black Market Nubian, or similar org directories

**Where They Congregate:**

*Online:*
- **Instagram** — @artsinboston (26K followers), @afhboston, individual artist accounts; hashtags: #BostonArt, #RoxburyArt, #BlackArtBoston, #BlackArtistsOfBoston
- **Facebook Groups** — "Boston Artists" (active local group), "Boston Black Business Network"
- **Eventbrite Boston** — listing and attending exhibitions, pop-ups, and workshops
- **BAMS Fest community channels** — artists who've applied or attended
- **BlackArtistsOfBoston.org** — Northeastern University project documenting the ecosystem

*Offline:*
- **BAMS Fest** (annual, Franklin Park) — 10,600+ attendees, Black art and music festival
- **ICA Boston events** — *Say It Loud: AAMARP 1977 to Now* runs through August 2, 2026
- **MFA Boston Curators Circles: Black Arts and Artists** — recurring evening events (next: June 25, 2026)
- **NCAAA (National Center of Afro-American Artists)** — Roxbury, recurring programming
- **Black Market Nubian, Nubian Square** — community art market
- **Open studios in South End, Dorchester, Roxbury**

**Disqualifiers (Not a Fit):**
- Artist based outside Boston metro (MVP is hyper-local; do not onboard nationally before product-market fit)
- Artist who only sells prints or digital files (MVP is focused on original physical artworks)
- Artist who has gallery representation and routes all sales through the gallery
- Collector or patron who is the primary persona (they are welcome but not the primary GTM target in months 1–6)

## 4. Channel Strategy

### Channel 1 — Physical Events (In-Person Community Entry)

**Goal:** Leads (artist signups) and brand awareness in the Boston Black arts community

**Tactics:**
- Attend 2–4 existing Boston art events monthly with a tablet or phone showing the app — BAMS Fest, open studios, gallery openings, MFA Curators Circles events
- Host 2–3 dedicated FrontStreet pop-up events at venues in Roxbury, South End, and Cambridge — live artist profile setup, artwork photo sessions, and collector demos
- Place QR codes linking to the waitlist or app at each event — track conversion by event
- Offer to photograph and professionally list artists' work at launch events in exchange for joining the platform
- Document every event with photo and video content for reuse across all other channels

**Cadence:** 2–4 events attended per month; 1 hosted event per month starting in launch month

**Tools:** QR code generator (free, e.g. QR Code Generator or Canva), phone/camera, Tally intake form (free)

**Success Metric:** Artist signups per event — target 3–5 artists per attended event, 8–12 per hosted event

**Budget:** $100–150/mo (venue rental/contribution for hosted events, printing for signage)

---

### Channel 2 — Instagram (Build in Public + Artist Spotlights)

**Goal:** Awareness and community recruitment

**Tactics:**
- Post 4x/week: mix of artist spotlights (feature a Boston Black artist's work with their permission), behind-the-scenes development updates, event announcements, and "did you know" posts about commission rates and platform comparisons
- Use Reels (30–60 seconds) for higher algorithmic reach — artist process videos, "a day with a Boston artist," app UI walkthroughs
- Actively comment (not just like) on Boston Black artist posts and arts organisation accounts — genuine engagement, not promotional spam
- Run a "feature request" Story poll series every 2 weeks — surfaces product feedback and signals that the community shapes the platform
- Repost artists who use the platform from the FrontStreet account — every repost is earned social proof

**Cadence:** 4 posts/week (2 feed, 2 Stories), 20–30 minutes of active community engagement daily

**Tools:** Buffer (Essentials — $6/mo for Instagram + 2 other channels), Canva Pro ($10/mo annually)

**Success Metric:** Waitlist signups from Instagram link in bio — target 10/month by Month 2, 30/month by Month 6

**Budget:** $16/mo (Buffer + Canva)

---

### Channel 3 — Email Newsletter (Beehiiv Free)

**Goal:** Retention and conversion of waitlist to active users

**Tactics:**
- Publish a bi-weekly email: "The FrontStreet Dispatch" — each issue features one Boston Black artist (spotlight with photos and a link to their profile), upcoming Boston art events, and one platform update or milestone
- Include a personal note from George in each issue — keeps the voice authentic and community-connected
- Segment the list by role (artist vs. collector/enthusiast) after Week 4 and personalise the CTAs accordingly
- Use the newsletter to announce beta access, launch dates, and new artist profiles before posting publicly
- Include a "refer a friend" prompt in each issue — artists know other artists

**Cadence:** Every 2 weeks (sustainable for solo founder)

**Tools:** Beehiiv (free up to 2,500 subscribers — sufficient through Month 6 at minimum)

**Success Metric:** Email list size and open rate — target 200 subscribers by Month 3, 40%+ open rate

**Budget:** $0 (Beehiiv free tier)

---

### Channel 4 — Institutional Partnerships

**Goal:** Leads (artist signups) via trusted referrals from within the community

**Tactics:**
- Identify and personally contact 5–8 Boston Black arts organisations (BAMS Fest, NCAAA, GRACC, AAMARP, Artists for Humanity, Castle of our Skins, Black Market Nubian, Roxbury International Film Festival) with a partnership proposal: FrontStreet as the recommended digital platform for their artist members
- Offer each organisation a co-branded landing page or featured section in the app for their artists
- Propose a revenue share on boosted listings or Pro subscriptions for artists referred by each org (to be implemented once monetisation is live)
- Apply for Massachusetts Cultural Council grant (deadline April 30, 2026) — the application process creates relationships with the MCC network
- Present at one Boston startup/arts event (MassChallenge, Smarter In The City, local arts council) per quarter

**Cadence:** 1–2 new partnership conversations per week; follow-up cycle of 2 weeks

**Tools:** Email (free), LinkedIn for research, Google Docs for proposal drafting

**Success Metric:** 2 confirmed institutional partnerships within 3 months of launch, generating 20+ artist referrals

**Budget:** $0 (relationship-based, no paid spend)

---

### Channel 5 — App Store Optimisation (ASO)

**Goal:** Organic discovery from artists actively searching for art marketplace apps

**Tactics:**
- Write App Store and Google Play listings using high-intent keywords: "black art app," "art portfolio app Boston," "sell art online," "art marketplace app"
- Use all available screenshot slots (10 on App Store) showing the most compelling product moments: artist profile, artwork browse feed, purchase flow
- Solicit 10+ initial reviews from beta users before public launch — reviews are the primary ranking signal
- Update store listing copy after Week 8 based on which features early users mention most in feedback

**Cadence:** One-time setup; update with each major release

**Tools:** App Store Connect and Google Play Console (included in developer accounts)

**Success Metric:** Organic app installs (no paid UA) — target 15% of total installs from organic store search by Month 3

**Budget:** $0

## 5. Sales Process

**Sales Model:** Self-serve with founder-assisted onboarding for the first 50 artists. Given the $0–7/mo price point, there is no demo-first or enterprise sales motion. Artists discover FrontStreet through events or community referrals, join via the app or web, and complete onboarding independently. The founder's job in Months 1–3 is to eliminate every friction point in that flow and to personally help the first cohort get their profiles fully populated — because a sparse profile converts no collectors.

**Pipeline Stages:**

| Stage | Definition | Actions | Exit Criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aware** | Artist has heard of FrontStreet (event, referral, Instagram) | None required — they come to us | Visits landing page or app store listing |
| **Interested** | Submits waitlist form or downloads app | Send welcome email via Beehiiv within 24hrs; include "here's what to do first" | Creates an account |
| **Onboarding** | Has created an account but profile <50% complete | Personal outreach from George (DM or email) offering to help complete profile; share profile setup guide | Profile has bio, photo, and ≥3 artwork listings |
| **Active Free** | Profile complete, artwork listed, not yet on Pro | Regular touchpoints via newsletter; feature their work in FrontStreet's Instagram if standout quality | Either first sale or Pro subscription |
| **First Sale** | First artwork purchase through platform | Trigger: post-sale email from George congratulating them and asking for testimonial | Continued listing behaviour; candidate for Pro |
| **Pro (Paid)** | Subscribed to Artist Pro ($7/mo) | Continue newsletter; offer early access to v2 features (events); invite to advisory community | Sustained monthly subscription |
| **Advocate** | Refers 2+ other artists or gives public testimonial | Feature in newsletter, Instagram spotlight; invite to events as "featured artist" | Ongoing organic referrals |

**Lead Scoring (Simple):**

| Signal | Points | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Submitted Tally artist intake form | +15 | Tally |
| Attended a FrontStreet event in person | +20 | Event check-in |
| Referred by a partner organisation (BAMS Fest, NCAAA, etc.) | +20 | UTM / intake form |
| Has >1,000 Instagram followers | +10 | Manual check |
| Tags work as "available for sale" on Instagram | +15 | Manual check during discovery |
| Opened 3+ FrontStreet newsletter emails | +10 | Beehiiv analytics |
| Completed full profile (bio + 5+ artworks) | +20 | Supabase dashboard |
| Connected Stripe account | +25 | Stripe / Supabase |
| Purchased Pro subscription | +30 | Stripe |
| Disqualifier: Based outside Boston metro | -50 | Intake form / profile |

*Score 40+: Priority personal follow-up from George within 48 hours*

**Follow-Up Cadence:**

| Day | Channel | Action | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Email (Beehiiv) | Welcome email: "You're on the list — here's what FrontStreet is building" | Set expectations; link to landing page |
| Day 2 | Instagram DM (if known) | Personal DM from @frontstreetapp: "Saw you signed up — any questions?" | Human touch; gather early feedback |
| Day 7 | Email | "Artist spotlight" newsletter issue — feature someone from their community | Build habit of opening FrontStreet emails |

| Day 14 | Email | "FrontStreet is live (or: beta is open)" — direct CTA to create profile | Activation |

## 6. Content Strategy

**Content Pillars:**

1. **Artist Spotlight** — Celebrating individual Boston Black artists, their work, and their story. Demonstrates the quality of artists on the platform and signals to collectors what they'll discover. Also functions as recruitment content — artists want to be featured.

2. **The Business of Being an Artist** — Practical content about selling art, commission rates, pricing work, Eventbrite fees vs. alternatives, and how to build collector relationships. Positions FrontStreet as an informed ally, not just a marketplace.

3. **Boston Black Arts Ecosystem** — What's happening in Boston: upcoming exhibitions, open studios, BAMS Fest updates, ICA programming, grant deadlines. FrontStreet becomes a trusted source of community information, not just a product account.

4. **Building in Public** — Transparent updates about building the platform: milestones hit, features shipped, decisions made. This pillar is unique to a founder building for their own community — it builds trust and turns the audience into invested stakeholders.

**Content Types by Funnel Stage:**

| Stage | Content Type | Topic Examples | Distribution Channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TOFU** | Instagram Reel | "Why Black artists lose 40% on Saatchi Art," "How to price your original artwork," "5 Boston art events this month" | Instagram, TikTok (repost) |
| **TOFU** | Newsletter (public issue) | "Artist Spotlight: [Name]," "The Boston Black arts calendar for [Month]" | Beehiiv, link shared on Instagram |
| **MOFU** | Instagram carousel | "FrontStreet vs. Eventbrite: the fee breakdown," "What artists are saying about FrontStreet," "How the app works" | Instagram |
| **MOFU** | Email (to waitlist) | "Beta is opening — here's how to get early access," "Meet the first 5 artists on FrontStreet" | Beehiiv |
| **BOFU** | Direct outreach content | "Your profile is 80% complete — here's the last step," "One artist made $X in their first month" | Email (personal), Instagram DM |
| **BOFU** | In-app onboarding tips | Supabase Edge Function-triggered welcome tips after signup | Email (Resend), in-app notification (v2) |
| **Retention** | Pro feature announcement | "Artists on Pro now get priority placement — here's how it works" | Email, Instagram Stories |
